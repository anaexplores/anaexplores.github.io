    Agent Reliability Calculator

    Shows why adding more verifiers stops helping after a point and why retrying an agent buys less than the usual formula promises. The defaults come from 131,332 real agent runs on four public benchmarks.

    browser tool4 public benchmarks
    Live Repo
    Details

    What it does

    It has four blocks. The first shows the reliability ceiling of a chain of steps with verifiers. The second compares naive retries with real ones. The third compares strategies by total cost. The fourth measures the repeat correlation (ICC) from your own CSV in the browser, and the file is never uploaded.

    What the data says

    Retries of the same agent on the same task are strongly correlated, with a median ICC of 0.49 to 0.67 across the corpora. A model fit on part of the data predicts the rest within 0.3 to 1.5 points, while the independence formula misses by 14 to 58.

    Honest limits

    ICC is not a universal constant. Across domains and models it ranges from 0.03 to 0.83, which is why you can measure your own.

    Honest agent scores

    Asks whether broken benchmark tasks or repeated runs change which AI agent comes out on top. On 3,300 Terminal-Bench 4.0 runs the answer was mostly no. Broken tasks can still make agents look different in reliability when they are not.

    reproducibleTerminal-Bench 4.0
    Repo
    Details

    What it checks

    Two common claims in AI agent evaluation. One is that benchmark defects hide the true leader, the other is that ranking by reliability on repeats (pass^5) reorders agents. The data are public per-trial results for 10 configurations on 66 tasks with 5 trials each.

    What the data says

    Neither change moves the leader beyond noise. Broken tasks can create false differences in reliability between agents. With 66 tasks, two agents within about 10 points of each other are not reliably ordered, and about 500 tasks are needed to resolve 5 points.

    Honest limits

    The test has low power against a shift of one or two places. The analysis plan was written before each analysis, and exploratory results are reported separately. A small tool runs the same checks on your own leaderboard.

    The Signature Explorer

    Explains digital signatures from the one in your crypto wallet to the post-quantum ones. You can sign a message, break it and even forge a signature yourself. Every fact on the page links to its original source.

    interactive explainerpost-quantum
    Live Repo
    Details

    What you can do

    Sign a message, tamper with it and watch verification fail. Reuse an ECDSA nonce on purpose and recover the private key from two signatures. Sign twice with one Lamport key and forge a message nobody signed, checked by the real verifier.

    Where it goes

    From there it follows the hash-based path through XMSS and FORS to SLH-DSA, standardised as FIPS 205. It runs entirely in the browser with no backend, and there are exercises for teachers.

    Honest limits

    An exercise completion code proves that a valid solution exists for that seed, not who produced it. The exercises are meant for practice, not proctored assessment.
